Checklist · read & respond

What it does

It is a checklist card, not a manual. Everything on the page is the line you are on, the lines behind you, and the phase you are in.

Flows vs checklists

The real distinction

Panel scans done from memory are marked as flows; read-and-respond checklists are marked as checklists — the same split a real crew makes. Airbus cards keep their printed below-the-line break.

Keyboard first

Space, and next

Space checks the highlighted line and moves on, so you are not hunting a mouse mid-departure. Arrows move, backspace undoes, N and P change phase.

Day & night

Two cockpits

A laminated paper card by day, a backlit EFB at night. Green means done, amber is the line you are on, cyan is the response — the colour language of the flight deck.

Stays put

Remembers the flight

Checked lines, the phase you left off on and the variant you picked are kept per aircraft in your browser. Close the tab mid-cruise and come back to it.

Your numbers

The blanks fill in

Every ____ on a card — V-speeds, QNH, fuel, minima, decision height — is a field you type into, kept with the rest of the flight. Reset flight clears them for the next one.

On paper

Print a kneeboard card

Print gives you the whole type — every phase, notes and all — laid out two columns to a page, black on white whichever theme you fly in, with the boxes left empty to tick by hand.

Fleet

Airliners first, plus the regional turboprops, business jets and transports people actually load up in a simulator. Variants that share a cockpit share a checklist and appear as chips carrying what differs; where the flight deck genuinely differs, the type gets its own list. Open the type menu and start typing to find one.
